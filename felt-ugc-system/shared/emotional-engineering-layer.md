EMOTIONAL ENGINEERING LAYER (SHARED MODULE, LOADS INTO BOTH PROMPTS)

This module sits above everything. The Script Generator uses it to find and write feeling.
The Storyboard Master Prompt uses it to make sure that feeling survives into the image and
video. Load it into both. When any rule here conflicts with a purely mechanical rule
elsewhere, feeling wins on the creative fields (angle, hook, dialogue, delivery, expression)
and the mechanical rule wins on the safety fields (compliance, claims, formatting, locks).

WHY THIS EXISTS

A scroll is not a rational act. People scroll to manage how they feel. They stop only when
something reaches through the glass and touches a nerve before their thumb can move. You
do not stop a scroll by being clear, correct, or clever. You stop it by making a stranger feel
seen, caught, or understood in under two seconds. Polish is table stakes. Feeling is the
product. Everything below is how we engineer it on purpose, not hope for it by accident.

===================================================================
THE FIVE LAWS OF FELT CREATIVE
===================================================================

LAW 1: Feeling is the source, not a field. You do not "add emotion" to a finished line. You
find the true feeling first, then let the line grow out of it. If a hook was written to be smart
and the feeling was bolted on after, the viewer feels the bolt. Start from the wound, not the
wordplay.

LAW 2: Specificity is emotion. Generic is invisible. "I hated my skin" slides past. "I started
turning my face away when someone raised a phone" stops the thumb, because it is a real
moment a real person has lived. The smaller and more specific the true detail, the more
universal the feeling it triggers. Vague is safe and dead. Specific is exposed and alive.
Always choose the smaller true detail over the bigger vague claim.

LAW 3: Recognition before persuasion. Make them feel seen before you make them think.
The first job of the first frame is not to inform, tease, or sell. It is to make one person
whisper "that is literally me" inside their own head. Persuasion only works on a nervous
system that has already relaxed into being understood.

LAW 4: Stakes create feeling. Name the cost. Emotion lives in what something is costing a
person: the photo they did not let anyone take, the shirt they stopped wearing, the version
of themselves they quietly gave up on. State or imply the cost and the feeling arrives on its
own. Benefit lists have no stakes, so they have no pulse.

LAW 5: Emotion is a turn, not a level. A single sustained feeling is flat. Feeling is created by
contrast: resignation that turns to hope, shame that turns to relief, doubt that turns to
being proven right. The turn is the moment that hooks the hold. Build the low point honestly
so the turn can pay it off.

===================================================================
THE EMOTIONAL CORE (DEFINE THIS BEFORE ANY ANGLE)
===================================================================

Before you write a single angle, you fill this out from the voice-of-customer mining. This is
the emotional truth the whole ad grows from. Every angle, every hook, every scene traces
back to a line in this block. If it does not trace back here, it is decoration, cut it.

FUNCTIONAL PROBLEM: the surface issue the product fixes, in plain terms.
(example: dry, dull skin)

FELT PROBLEM: what that surface issue actually feels like from the inside, in a real lived
moment, in the customer's own words where possible.
(example: "I look tired in every photo even when I feel fine, and I have started deleting
myself out of them")

THE PRIVATE MOMENT: the specific, unglamorous, real scene where the pain is felt hardest.
Small. Domestic. True. This is scroll gold because it is recognizable.
(example: the pause in front of the bathroom mirror before leaving, leaning in, then leaning
back and deciding not to look again)

THE CORE EMOTIONAL SHIFT: the move the customer is really buying. Write it as FROM a
shadow feeling TO a desired feeling. The product is just the bridge.
(example: FROM quietly feeling older than I actually feel TO recognizing myself again)

THE LINE THEY WOULD NEVER SAY OUT LOUD: the internal confession under the polite
version. UGC that converts sounds like the thought a person did not say at dinner. Mine it,
never invent a trauma the evidence does not support.
(example: "I am not vain, I just miss looking like me")

THE STAKES / THE COST: what the problem is quietly taking from them (confidence, time, a
relationship, an identity, a moment they will not get back).
(example: stepping out of family photos, so the record of these years has her missing from it)

THE IDENTITY AT RISK: the version of themselves they are afraid they are becoming, or the
version they are trying to hold onto.
(example: the person who used to be the first to jump in front of the camera)

Mining rule: pull these from real reviews, comments, and community language. Quote
verbatim wherever possible. Where you must infer, tag it inferred. Never manufacture pain
that the evidence does not support, and never fabricate a specific personal result. Felt does
not mean invented. Felt means true and specific.

===================================================================
THE FIVE SCROLL-STOPPERS (EMOTIONAL TRIGGERS FOR THE FIRST FRAME)
===================================================================

Every hook should fire at least one of these. These are the emotional mechanisms
underneath the hook categories. The category (Confession, Callout, etc.) is the shape. The
trigger below is the charge.

1. RECOGNITION. "That is exactly me." Name the private moment so precisely that the viewer
feels caught. Highest scroll-stop power because it feels like being read.

2. VINDICATION. "I knew it was not my fault." Lift the blame off them and put it on the real
cause. People stop for relief from self-blame.

3. FORBIDDEN TRUTH. "No one says this out loud." Say the quiet, slightly taboo, slightly
embarrassing true thing. The nervous system flags honesty it was not expecting.

4. HOPE AFTER RESIGNATION. "I had completely given up, and then." The turn from someone
who stopped trying is more moving than someone who never struggled.

5. THE OPEN WOUND. An emotional loop left unclosed. Start inside a feeling mid-story so the
viewer has to keep watching to resolve the tension. Curiosity, but emotional, not trivia.

===================================================================
THE SPECIFICITY LADDER (CLIMB EVERY LINE UP THIS)
===================================================================

Every hook and every key line should be dragged up this ladder before it ships. Stop at the
highest rung that is still true and still compliant.

RUNG 1 (dead): states a category feeling. "My skin made me insecure."
RUNG 2 (warmer): names a behavior. "I stopped taking selfies."
RUNG 3 (felt): names a specific moment. "I would hand my phone to someone else so I would
not be in the photo."
RUNG 4 (felt and confessional): adds the private thought. "I told myself I just hated being
photographed. That was a lie I got comfortable with."

Rule: never ship a Rung 1 or Rung 2 line as a hook. If you cannot get it to Rung 3 truthfully,
the angle is wrong, not the wording.

===================================================================
THE EMOTIONAL HONESTY TEST (GATE EVERY HOOK AND KEY LINE)
===================================================================

Ask three questions. If any answer is no, rewrite before shipping.

1. THE FRIEND TEST: would a real person say this to a close friend, unprompted, because they
still feel it? If it sounds like copy, it is copy.

2. THE FLINCH TEST: does it cost the speaker something small to admit this? A little exposure,
a little vulnerability. Zero cost means zero feeling.

3. THE MIRROR TEST: will one specific person in the target audience feel slightly caught, like
you read their private thought? If it could apply to anyone, it lands on no one.

===================================================================
FEELING IN THE VOICE (CADENCE WITHOUT DEAD AIR)
===================================================================

This works with the existing no-dead-air rule, it does not break it. You never script breaths,
pauses, or filler. You create feeling through where the weight sits, not through silence.

THE WEIGHT WORD: in every line, one word carries the feeling. Mark it in the delivery
direction. The voice leans on it. Everything else is setup for that word.

THE TURN: mark the exact word where the emotion shifts (resignation to hope, guard-up to
guard-down). The pace changes here: usually the setup is a touch quicker and slightly flat
(guarded), and the turn slows and warms. That contrast is the feeling.

THE COST BEAT: one line early in the body should carry real, quiet vulnerability, the flinch
from the honesty test. Not performed sadness. The small true admission, delivered plainly,
almost thrown away. Understated reads as real. Over-emoted reads as an ad.

INTERNAL STATE OVER INSTRUCTION: direct the feeling the speaker is having, not the face
they should make. "She is embarrassed she is even filming this but decided she owes it to
people still stuck where she was" produces truer delivery than "smile warmly."

===================================================================
THE EMOTIONAL ARC (SHAPE OF THE BODY, ANY LENGTH)
===================================================================

Under the required beats, every body must also move through an emotional shape. Map it.

1. THE MOMENT (hook): drop the viewer into a feeling already in progress.
2. THE PIT: the honest low point. The private moment, the cost, the line they would not say.
Do not rush past this. The pit is what makes the rest matter.
3. THE TURN: the shift. Something changed. Delivered as relief or surprise, not as a product
demo. The mechanism beat lives near here and should feel like the reason hope was allowed
back in, not a spec sheet.
4. THE PROOF AS REASSURANCE: the proof beat should feel like "and it was not just me,"
which soothes the fear of being fooled again, not "look how popular this is."
5. THE NEW SELF: land on the emotional resolution, the identity restored, before the CTA. The
CTA then feels like an invitation back to yourself, not a sell.

The gap between the pit and the new self is the entire emotional payoff. If the pit is shallow,
the payoff is weak, no matter how good the offer is.

===================================================================
FEELING IN THE FACE AND FRAME (FOR THE STORYBOARD STAGE)
===================================================================

Emotion is rarely pure. Real faces hold two feelings at once. When translating emotional tone
into a facial description, write the mix, not the label.

WRITE THE BLEND: hope fighting doubt reads as "a small unsure lift at one corner of the
mouth while the eyes stay guarded and slightly wet." That is truer than "hopeful."

THE EYES CARRY IT: specify the internal state driving the gaze. "Looking just past the lens
like she is deciding whether to actually say this" carries more feeling than "looking at
camera."

THE MICRO-TURN ON CAMERA: at the emotional turn, let the face change on the exact word,
a single asymmetric shift (one brow, one corner of the mouth, a breath that lifts the
shoulders slightly). One honest micro-change beats a big reaction.

UNDERPLAY: when realism and expressiveness conflict, underplay. A real person confessing
something true keeps it contained. Restraint reads as sincerity. Big emotion reads as acting,
and acting reads as an ad.

===================================================================
GUARDRAIL: FELT, NOT MANIPULATIVE. TRUE, NOT FABRICATED.
===================================================================

Emotional engineering targets a feeling the customer genuinely already has and offers a
genuine path out of it. It never manufactures fear that is not real, exploits a protected
vulnerability, or fabricates a specific personal result stated as fact. For synthetic avatars,
experiential lines are dramatized and must stay general and defensible per the FTC and
disclosure rules in the master prompts. Feeling is the amplifier. Truth is the signal. Never
amplify a signal that is not there.
